This manual describes version 0.30.3 of GP-200 Studio Web. The version number and build date are shown at the bottom of the app: that is how to check you are using the right one.

1Welcome

GP-200 Studio Web is a preset workshop for the Valeton GP-200, GP-150 and GP-180 multi-effects pedals. It runs in your browser: nothing to install, nothing to download except the page itself.

What you can do

  • Describe a sound (“Enter Sandman – Metallica, from the clean intro to the solo”) and get ready-to-play presets: 3 variants for each section of the song.
  • Open your existing presets (.prst files), look at them module by module, tweak them, refine them with AI.
  • Send them to the pedal over USB, without the official editor, and tweak the pedal live while you play.
  • See and organize the pedal memory: all 256 slots with their names, and rearrange them by drag and drop.
  • Prepare a gig: keep your favorites ♥, name them, arrange them song by song in the pedal banks, send everything in one go.
  • Bring every preset to the same loudness (automatic LUFS measurement), so none of them jumps out from one song to the next.

What you need

BrowserChrome, Edge or Opera on a computer (or Chrome on Android, see the chapter “Connecting the pedal”). Safari, Firefox, iPhone and iPad cannot connect to the pedal: you can still create and download presets there.
AddressThe page must be opened from its https address (for example on GitHub Pages) or from localhost.
PedalPlugged into the computer over USB. Only one program at a time can use the pedal: close the Valeton editor when you use GP-200 Studio.
AI keyOnly needed to generate and refine presets. Everything else (open, send, match volumes, set list…) works without a key.
Your privacy. Everything happens in your browser. Your AI key is sent only to the provider you chose (never to a GP-200 Studio server) and the page loads no external font, script or tracker. The .prst files are built on the spot.

How to read this manual

The screenshots carry numbered orange markers 1: the number points to the caption just below. Four kinds of boxes come back regularly:

Tip: a shortcut, a good habitWarning: an action that overwrites somethingSafety: what the app does to protect youNot tested on hardware: verified with a simulated pedal only

2Quick start

Five minutes to go from “nothing” to “a preset sounding in the pedal”.

  1. Open the page in Chrome or Edge. On first launch, a window offers to connect an AI: paste your key (chapter 4) or click Continue without AI.
  2. Plug in the pedal over USB, switch it on, then click the Connect the pedal pill at the top right. Accept the browser’s MIDI permission.
  3. Choose your working slot: a slot you are willing to sacrifice (for example 50-D). Every preset you audition will be written there. Never choose a slot that holds a preset you want to keep.
  4. Describe the sound in the big field and click Generate presets. After a few seconds, the presets appear on the left, and the first one is already in your pedal.
  5. Play, tweak, keep. Click another preset to hear it, turn a setting (it moves live on the pedal), put a ♥ on the ones you keep, then arrange them with the Set list (chapter 10).
The working slot is overwritten on every audition. Nothing is written to the pedal until you confirm that slot. The other slots are never touched, unless you explicitly send a preset or a set list and accept the confirmation.

3The screen, area by area

On a computer, everything fits on one screen, with no page scrolling. Here is the full screen with a pedal connected.

  1. Mode: New song (generate) or Refine an existing preset.
  2. Open a .prst: loads one or several files (you can also drop them anywhere on the page).
  3. AI pill: provider and model; a click reopens the connection window.
  4. Pedal pill: a click connects the pedal (automatic reconnection afterwards); once connected, it opens the Pedal tab.
  5. Working slot (📍 50-D): the slot where every auditioned preset goes.
  6. Live: the live-editing switch (chapter 6).
  7. Pedal memory: the 256 slots, with their names (chapter 9).
  8. My presets: everything you generate or open piles up here; ♥ = favorite; right-click = menu.
  9. Request area: the field where you describe the sound (or what you want to change).
  10. Rack: the chain of 11 modules of the displayed preset, with the patch volume above.
  11. Tabs: Result, Pedal, CTRL, Volume, Set list, Log. They open in a floating window (close: ✕, Esc or a click outside). Under the tabs: the sheet of the selected module.
  12. User manual (?): downloads this manual (PDF), in the language of the interface.
  13. Report a problem and the version number (bottom).
Tooltips everywhere. Every button, field, slider and indicator has a tooltip (hover it): what the setting does, its range, its default value. When in doubt, hover. The interface exists in French and English (FR / EN buttons at the top right).

4Connecting an AI

The AI does two things: generate presets from a description, and refine an existing preset. Without a key, you keep everything else.

  1. Provider and model: Google Gemini, Anthropic or OpenRouter. (OpenAI and Perplexity do not allow direct calls from a browser.) The Refresh list button reads the available models again. One-click OpenRouter connection requires the page to be served over https.
  2. API key: paste it here (the Show button reveals it). The Get a key links and the 3 steps under the title explain where to create one. The “Remember the key” box keeps it in this browser: untick it on a shared computer.
  3. OK: confirms. It stays greyed out until there is a key.
  4. Continue without AI: you can open presets, send them, match volumes, use the set list and the memory. Only “Generate” and “Refine” need a key. You can add one later with the Connect an AI pill.
Free Gemini key. Free keys are served after paid keys when the service is busy. If Gemini answers “overloaded” after several tries, the app switches by itself to a flash-lite model for the current generation.
An “odd-looking” key is not rejected. The app does not check the format of your key (there are several). Only the provider knows whether it is valid: if it refuses it, you will see “The API key is refused by the provider” at the first generation.

5Creating presets with AI

Describe a song, an artist, a mood: the AI identifies the rig, splits the song into sections (intro, verse, solo…) and generates 3 variants per section.

Writing a good request

  • Most effective: title – artist and, if you like, a precision: “Nothing Else Matters – Metallica, from the clean intro to the solo”.
  • The examples under the field are clickable and fill in the request.
  • You can also describe a sound without a song: “warm blues, a bit of reverb, single-coil pickups”.

Generation options

Under the preset list, the Generation options panel sets what the AI must produce:

  • Structure: Auto-detect (the AI decides: 1 sound = 3 presets, 2 sounds = 6, 3 sounds = 9) or an imposed type: clean, crunch, dist, lead.
  • Your guitar pickups: humbucker, single-coil, P90, active (EMG…), or “Unspecified”. The AI takes them into account for gain and EQ.
  • Web search: asks the AI to look up the real gear used by the artist, when the provider allows it.

During generation, the rack LEDs light up one after the other: this is normal (turned off if your system asks to reduce animations).

The Result tab

It sums up what the AI understood: the sound of the displayed section, the identified rig, the structure of the song. After a refinement, it also shows the actual changes between the old and the new version.

Keeping your presets

  • Download this preset (.prst): a file ready to be opened by the Valeton editor or reopened here later.
  • Download all (.zip): all the presets of the result, with a detail .json per preset.
  • Presets stay in “My presets” as long as the page is open. They do not survive closing the tab: download or send what matters.

6Opening and tweaking a preset

The rack shows the full chain of a preset. Each module is adjusted with the mouse, within the pedal’s real ranges.

Opening files

Click Open a .prst (several files at once is fine) or drop them anywhere on the page. An invalid file is reported without blocking the others. Opened presets get the “opened” badge in the list.

The rack and the module sheet

  1. Patch volume (0 to 100): the “patch volume” knob of the front panel. It applies to the whole preset, not to one module.
  2. Module: a first click selects it (focus). One more click on the already selected module turns it on or off. The ← → arrows move from one module to the next.
  3. LED: module bypass (a single click, always). If the pedal is connected, you see and hear it live.
  4. Model: the list follows the pedal’s real menu for this block (for example 31 models in PRE).
  5. State: “active” or “bypass”.
  6. Settings: slider or keyboard entry in the pill; at most 6 per row. The measured level of the amp or the drive is shown under the title.
  7. Send this module to the pedal: sends the whole module again (model and all its values).
  8. Save on the pedal…: opens the Pedal tab to write the preset into a slot (chapter 8).

Live editing

When the pedal is connected and the Live switch is on, every slider move, model change or bypass goes straight to the pedal. These settings are temporary: until you write the preset into a slot, they disappear if you change patch on the pedal. Turn Live off so that only the file on screen changes.

The other way too. If you turn a knob on the pedal’s front panel, or press a module footswitch, the page follows: the slider, the LED and the file update. The patch volume is followed the same way.
The ↻ symbol next to a setting means it is “silent live”: the pedal only takes it into account when the preset is loaded (a few Sync, Trail, Spread and mode settings). Send the module again or write the preset to hear it.

The right-click menu on a preset

In “My presets”, a right-click (long press on touch, or the Menu / Shift+F10 key) offers:

  • Duplicate: creates an independent copy right below (“Name 2”, “Name 3”…), to try a boost without touching the original.
  • Rename…: changes the name (16 characters at most on GP-200, 13 on GP-150/180, accents removed) and the file name.
  • Revert to original: restores the preset as it was when created (for a copy: as it was at the time of duplication, matched volume included).
  • Delete this copy: on copies only, never on a generated or opened preset.

Copies can be favorited, exported, volume-matched and sent like the others. The menu is unavailable during volume matching or a send.

7Refining a preset

A preset is almost right? Tell the AI what is wrong, in plain English. It corrects the selected preset and adds the result to the list, the original is kept.

  • Choose the Refine an existing preset mode, select the preset in the list (generated, opened or already refined), then write: “too much reverb, it needs a flanger”, “not enough gain, the sound is dull in the highs”, “add a dotted-eighth delay”.
  • Refine the preset sends the request. The new preset arrives in the list (“refined” badge) and the Result tab shows the actual changes.
  • 🎤 Optimize for live changes no effect: it only adjusts gain, mix, EQ and cab to play loud, in a band.
Your settings are not lost. Refining keeps the CTRL table and the patch volume of the original file, as well as the models already in the preset (even outside the current menu). Only a new model that the pedal does not offer in this block is refused and sent back to the AI for correction.

8Connecting the pedal

Connection, working slot, listening to the pedal and sending presets.

Connecting

  1. Close the Valeton editor if it is open (only one program at a time can use the pedal).
  2. Plug the pedal in over USB, switch it on.
  3. Click the Connect the pedal pill. The browser asks for permission to access MIDI devices with SysEx: accept. On later visits, reconnection is automatic.
  4. The pill turns green and shows the recognized model (GP-200, GP-150 or GP-180). On connection, the page reads the current patch and displays it.

Choosing the working slot

  1. Number (1 to 64) and letter (A to D): the working slot. The 📍 pill in the header (here 50-D) reminds you of it and reopens it at any time.
  2. Inject automatically as soon as a preset is chosen (click in the list, generation, opening, refinement result). Without this box, the 🔌 Inject button sends the preset on demand.
  3. OK: nothing is written to the pedal until you confirm.

The Listen to the pedal box (ticked by default) keeps the MIDI port open so the page follows what happens on the pedal: patch change, module toggle, knob turned on the front panel. Untick it to use the Valeton editor, which does not share the port.

The working slot is rewritten on every selection. Choose a slot you do not need. Injection writes the preset into this slot and then selects it: the pedal loads it from its memory, so every module has the right model and live settings target the right parameters.

The Pedal tab: sending presets

  1. Read the current patch: reads on demand the patch currently loaded on the pedal (unsaved settings included) and adds it to the list with the “pedal” badge. It is read-only: this patch is never sent back automatically.
  2. Number and letter of the destination slot.
  3. Send this preset to 12-B: writes the displayed preset into the chosen slot, after confirmation (“What you are about to overwrite”).
  4. Send the 3 variants: writes the displayed section (A, B, C) into three consecutive slots.

At the bottom, the What the pedal says panel keeps the last messages received in hexadecimal: useful for a problem report.

If the pedal changes patch. Live settings are suspended (nothing is sent to another patch) and a banner offers Send the preset again. If the pedal reloads the working slot while you had settings that were not injected, a banner points it out.
Discreet messages. When all is well (“GP-200 on 50-D · patch volume 57”, “Injected and selected…”), the information stays in the bottom bar. Alerts that need an action stay near the rack.

On Android (USB OTG cable)

Chrome for Android + an OTG adapter and the pedal’s USB-B cable (or a USB-C → USB-B cable) give the same sends and the same live editing. The page must be opened from its https address: opened from an attachment or a downloaded file, Chrome refuses MIDI. Sending is paced more cautiously there.

9The pedal memory

A column on the left lists the 256 slots (01-A to 64-D) with the name of the patch stored in each. You can browse them… and rearrange them.

Reading and browsing

  • Reading starts a few seconds after connection and takes about 6 seconds. The counter (256/256) shows progress. The ↻ button reads everything again.
  • A click on a slot selects it on the pedal (like with your foot), then the page reads it and shows it in the rack.
  • After an injection or a set list send, the affected slots are read again right away. On reconnection, names already read stay displayed (greyed) and are then read again.
  • Reading is read-only: no write is ever sent by simply browsing.

Rearranging by drag and drop (GP-200)

A single preset. Want to put 40-A in place of 42-B? Grab it and drag it onto it: the target row is underlined, it takes that place and the others shift by one slot (handy when the order of the songs in your set list changes). As soon as you drop it, a confirmation window opens (“Move “Sandman Intro” from 40-A to 42-B”). Nothing is written until you agree; Cancel puts everything back as it was. A simple click, without dragging, still loads the preset on the pedal.

Several presets. The move is first a plan, only displayed: you see the result before the pedal is touched (figure 11 shows one: two presets, 40-A and 40-B, dropped on 42-B).

  1. Plan banner: “10 slot(s) to rewrite. Nothing has been written to the pedal yet.”
  2. Apply to the pedal…: starts writing (after confirmation).
  3. Cancel: abandons the plan, everything goes back as before.
  4. Destination of the moved block (here 42-A and 42-B receive “Sandman Intro” and “Sandman Riff”).
  5. The others shift: italic lines with a left border are those that will be rewritten; hovering shows where each content comes from.

You can chain several drag-and-drops: each one is added to the plan, and a single send applies the whole.

Moving several presets at once

  • Ctrl (or ⌘) + click: adds or removes a slot.
  • Shift + click: selects a range.
  • Shift + click with no previous click: the range starts from the slot loaded on the pedal.
  • Drag one of the selected slots: the whole block moves, keeping its order (the image following the cursor shows the number of slots). Example: select 50-A → 54-D and drag them onto 01-A: they become 01-A → 05-D and the rest shifts.
  • To reach a distant slot, bring the cursor near the top or bottom of the list: it scrolls by itself, faster and faster near the edge.
  • Move the selection to [bank] [letter] Move: under the list, places the selected block where you want without dragging (same plan, same safeguards).
  • Alt + ↑ / ↓: shifts the selection by one step, from the keyboard (also the way to do it on a touch screen, where dragging is not supported).

During a plan, a simple click selects without loading anything (the displayed content is no longer the pedal’s).

Applying the plan

  1. The exact list of overwrites: “42-B ← 40-A P156”… each slot with the content it will receive. It has two groups: first what you moved, then the presets shifted to make room for them (they take the freed slots).
  2. Back up and apply.
  3. Cancel: nothing has been written.

Clicking Back up and apply runs five steps, with a progress bar:

  1. Reading of all the slots involved, with a name check. If one of them changed since it was displayed, everything stops before the slightest write (read again with ↻, then start over).
  2. ZIP backup (pedal_backup_YYYYMMDD_HHMM.zip, one .prst per slot) downloaded before the first write.
  3. Writing, slot by slot. Allow a few seconds per slot; do not unplug the pedal.
  4. Verification read of every written slot; those that differ are flagged “To check”.
  5. The pedal moves to the first modified slot.
If something goes wrong. If the cable is unplugged during the send, the plan is abandoned and the message says how many slots were written and which ones (the one in progress is marked “(?)”). The memory is read again, and the ZIP backup lets you restore everything: see below.

Restoring a backup

The ZIP downloaded before each rearrangement holds one .prst per slot, named after its slot (07-B_PresetName.prst). To go back:

  1. In the “Pedal memory” column, click Restore… (next to the 256/256 counter; enabled once the 256 names are read and no plan is pending).
  2. Choose the backup ZIP (or directly .prst files whose name starts with the slot). Files with no slot in their name, a wrong size or a bad checksum are ignored and reported.
  3. A window lists each slot, the preset it will receive and what it currently holds. Nothing is written until you click Back up the current state and restore.
  4. The page reads the current state, downloads it as a before_restore_… ZIP (so a restore can itself be undone), writes, then reads every slot back to check it.
Recent feature. The move was verified against a simulated pedal that really keeps the content of its 256 slots (byte by byte). Not yet on a real pedal: keep the ZIP backup at least the first time. Available on GP-200 only.

10Favorites, set list and gigs

You are preparing a gig: you audition dozens of presets, keep a few, name them, and arrange them in the pedal in set list order.

Keeping with the heart ♥

  1. Favorites bar, which appears as soon as a preset is kept: Export (.zip) (one .prst per favorite, numbered 01_, 02_… in set list order), Match volumes (opens the Volume window with the favorites only) and Set list.
  2. ♥ to the right of each preset: a click keeps or removes it. Keeping a preset does not change the selection: you keep auditioning.
The favorites list is kept only in the tab. If you close the page, it disappears: export (ZIP) first.

The Set list window

  1. Starting bank (1 to 64) and letter: the first slot to fill.
  2. Assign: consecutive slots starting from that one (50-A, 50-B, 50-C, 50-D, 51-A…).
  3. Assign by song: each song stays in a single bank (see below).
  4. The list: one name per preset (16 characters, accents replaced by the base letter: “Café” becomes “Cafe”), a bank and a letter, and the ↑ ↓ ✕ buttons to sort or remove.
  5. Send N preset(s) to the pedal: asks for a confirmation, then writes everything.
  6. Download the set list (ZIP): to keep it (the list is not kept after the page is closed).

“Assign by song”: one song, one bank

On stage, switching between presets of the same song without changing bank is much safer. The button groups the favorites by song (in order of first appearance) and fills letters A to D of each bank. A song never straddles two banks: if it does not fit in the remaining letters, it starts in the next bank.

Sending

  1. The list of overwritten slots, with what they will receive (“50-A ← Sandman Intro A”).
  2. Overwrite and send: written one by one, with a progress bar; the pedal moves to the first preset at the end.
  3. Cancel.
Checks before sending. Blocking: empty name, missing or invalid slot, two presets on the same slot, more than 256 presets. Warnings: modified name (accents removed), two identical names, slot equal to the working slot (it will be rewritten on every selection). In case of interruption (cable unplugged…), the message says how many presets were written and which ones.
Your files are never modified. The name you type exists only in the copy sent to the pedal (16 name bytes + recomputed checksum, nothing else).

11Matching volumes

A preset that is too loud or too quiet ruins a run of songs. Here, you play a chord on a loop: the app listens to the pedal’s output, measures the loudness in LUFS (the unit used by YouTube and Spotify) and adjusts by itself until it reaches the target.

What you need

  • The pedal connected over USB and its USB audio input (it shows up as a “GP-200” sound card): the browser asks for permission to use the microphone / audio input.
  • Your guitar plugged into the pedal.
  • The tab visible during the measurement: the browser slows down hidden tabs.
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer.

Preparing the batch

The window reads top to bottom: A the pedal, B the audio input (choose “GP-200”), C the working slot, D the presets and their target volume.

  1. The batch (step D): the list of everything that will be matched, in measurement order. You can mix generated, opened or refined presets. ↑ ↓ reorder, ✕ removes.
  2. Open .prst files…: adds several files at once. The window stays open and nothing is sent to the pedal before you start.
  3. ♥ Add favorites (6): puts all your favorites into the batch at once. A huge time saver before a gig.
  4. Add all: the whole library. The “Add presets to match” panel lets you add them one by one.
  5. Start matching (greyed out while the pedal or the audio input is missing).

For each preset, choose the type: Normal (−16 LUFS) for rhythm parts and clean sounds, Lead (−11 LUFS) for solos, which must sit above the mix. All presets go, one at a time, through the same working slot (the slot chosen at step C).

The working slot is overwritten during the whole operation: the app asks for confirmation (“Overwrite and start”). Choose an empty slot.

During the measurement

Play a chord on a loop, steadily, without changing your strength. The app first adjusts the amp volume, then the cab, then the patch volume, and gain as a last resort. As soon as the level is within tolerance (0.4 LU) for 3 stable seconds, the preset is validated and saved, then it moves on to the next one, with no click between two measurements.

Buttons: Pause, Send the preset again, Previous, Next, Keep this setting (validate as is), Stop.

The result

  • The page’s .prst files are updated (✓ in the library). They are not in the pedal yet: send them with the Pedal tab or the Set list.
  • Download all (zip): one .prst and one .json per preset in the batch.
Hardware. Measurement and adjustment are identical to the desktop program (same logic, same decisions), verified with a fake pedal and a fake microphone. On GP-150 / GP-180, the measurement constants are taken from the GP-200: every write is verified by reading back.

12CTRL: multi-effect footswitches

CTRL 1 to 8 are the preset’s “switches”: each one turns on or off, with a single press, the modules you tick.

  1. The grid: one row per CTRL, one column per module (PRE, WAH, DST, AMP, NR, CAB, EQ, MOD, DLY, RVB, VOL). Each ticked box is applied immediately to the displayed preset.
  2. The summary in plain words, here CTRL1=AMP+DLY, CTRL2=PRE.
  3. Send the preset to the pedal…: so that the pedal takes it into account.
Which footswitch sends which CTRL? This is set on the pedal, in Global > Footswitch, not in the app.

The CTRL tab does not exist on GP-150 / GP-180.

14On phone and tablet

Below 1100 px of width, the page stacks: request area, preset list, rack, module sheet.

  • Large touch targets are kept (main button 48 px).
  • Long press on a preset = right-click menu.
  • Drag and drop in the memory is not supported on touch: select a slot then use Alt + arrows with a keyboard, or do the rearranging from a computer.
  • To send presets from Android, plug the pedal in over USB OTG (chapter 8).
  • iPhone and iPad: no MIDI in the browser, but you can generate, open and download presets.

15Reporting a problem

A Report a problem link at the bottom of the page opens a diagnostic panel. It also opens by itself at the first unexpected error.

  • Nothing is sent automatically. Copy the report puts it on your clipboard; paste it in a message or in a GitHub “issue” (GitHub button). Details shows the full text.
  • The report contains: version, browser, AI provider and model, MIDI state (ports seen, port chosen, pedal patch) and the last 80 events.
  • No API key appears in it: keys (Google, Anthropic / OpenRouter sk-…, Bearer, api_key=…) are masked as [key hidden]. The address of a local page is masked too.
  • The Log tab keeps the course of the generation.

Glossary

Preset, patch
A complete sound: the 11 modules, their settings, the volume and the CTRLs. “Patch” is the pedal’s word, “preset” the app’s; they mean the same thing.
.prst
The file of a preset, in the Valeton editor’s format.
Slot
One of the pedal’s memory cells (01-A to 64-D on GP-200).
Working slot, injection slot
The slot the app rewrites for every auditioned preset.
Injection
Writing the preset into the working slot, then selecting it on the pedal.
Bypass
Module turned off (bypassed), the LED is off.
CTRL
A preset “switch” that turns several modules on or off at once.
IR / cab
Simulation of the speaker and the microphone (impulse response).
LUFS / LU
Units of perceived loudness. Target: −16 LUFS (normal), −11 LUFS (lead); the tolerance is 0.4 LU.
SysEx
MIDI “system exclusive” messages: they are what carries presets to the pedal. The browser asks for a special permission to use them.
Web MIDI
The browser feature that lets the page talk to the pedal over USB.

Known limits

  • Only Gemini, OpenRouter and Anthropic are offered (the other providers refuse calls from a browser).
  • The API key is remembered in the browser (box to untick on a shared computer).
  • USB sending, volume matching and memory rearranging were verified with a simulated pedal and microphone, byte by byte against the desktop program; reading and listening were also confirmed on a real GP-200 pedal.
  • The app is a free project, not affiliated with Valeton.
